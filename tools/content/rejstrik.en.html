What you may do with prints

Six rights classes

The label on each project follows the hardware licence, i.e. the printed parts and CAD files. Software and documentation may be under a different licence; each project lists the exact wording.

Permissive
You may print, modify and sell the parts. Usually you have to credit the author and the licence (e.g. MIT, Apache-2.0, CC BY, CERN-OHL-P).
Copyleft
You may print, modify and sell, but modified files have to be published under the same licence, with credit to the author (e.g. CC BY-SA, GPL, CERN-OHL-S/W).
Non-commercial only
Printing and modifying for yourself, a school or a club. Selling prints or kits needs the author’s permission (e.g. CC BY-NC).
No modifications
You may share the files only as they are. Don’t publish modified versions without the author’s permission (e.g. CC BY-ND).
Closed
The author reserves the rights. Printing for others, sharing modifications and selling only with their permission.
No licence stated
The project states no licence, or we have not been able to confirm one. Without a licence the author keeps all rights: you can look at the files, but sharing modified versions or selling prints needs their permission.

This is a simplified overview; the full licence text on the project is what counts. More in the guide Open-source hardware licences explained.

Some projects have strong motors, sharp parts or lithium batteries, and the lab projects handle liquids and biological material. Younger builders should work with an adult.
