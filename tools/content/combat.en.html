Getting started with 150 g combat robots

The 150 g class is called antweight in the UK and fairyweight in the US. The robots are small, cheap to repair and fit in a shoebox. In Czechia they compete in Czech Combat Robot League tournaments.

Rules change. The summary below is based on the rules published as of 29 October 2026. Before a tournament, always read the current rules (in Czech) and ask the organiser if anything is unclear.

01 — Arena first

In our 150 g arena you can see how a pusher, a flipper or a spinner behaves in a match. In the garage you change wheels, gears and weapons, and the weight is kept within 150 g. It is a simplified simulation, but it quickly shows why traction and a low centre of gravity matter.

02 — Rules

What the antweight rules say

For example, the Czech Combat Robot League vol. 4 tournament (28 June 2026, TechTower, Pilsen) was run by FLS Battlebots together with SIT Port Pilsen, in the antweight class only.1 From the rules of this class:2

  • Weight: 150 g. Robots without wheels, tracks or other rolling mechanisms may weigh 225 g; walking robots whose legs have at least two degrees of freedom, 300 g.
  • Size: the robot must fit in a 15 × 15 cm square; height is unlimited.
  • Failsafe is mandatory: when the transmitter is switched off or the signal is lost, the robot must stop all movement, including the weapon.
  • Switch: the robot must be switchable off from the outside (a switch, a removable link or the battery connector) with clearly marked positions.
  • Batteries: 24 V maximum. Charging must never be left unattended and takes place next to a non-flammable container or in a LiPo safety bag.
  • Weapons: banned weapons include nets and glue, liquids, electrical and jamming weapons, explosives and free-flying projectiles. Dangerous moving weapons need a reliable mechanical lock outside the arena.
  • Match: 3 minutes as standard. A robot loses if it falls out of the arena or cannot continue; a robot that doesn’t move gets a ten-second count.

This is a short summary, not a substitute for the rules. The text on the organiser’s website is binding.

03 — First robot

Why start with a pusher

A pusher has no spinning weapon. It wins on traction and driving: it pushes the opponent out of the arena or pins it against the wall. It is the safest and cheapest way to start, and it teaches you the essentials: the weight budget, wiring, failsafe and driving.

One example is Project SVRN by Shoverobotics (CC BY 4.0 licence): a four-wheel-drive pusher with four N10 motors, plus a wedge, flipper or gripper. It is controlled by a Malenki Nano HV (receiver and speed controllers on one board; the standard Nano is too weak for four N10 motors), driven with a FlySky transmitter and powered by a 2S LiPo battery. The original files and instructions are on Printables. Our kit is made from them; the author is not affiliated with us.

Spinners later. A spinning weapon can cause serious injury and throw debris. Build one once you have a few matches behind you, and only power it up in an enclosed arena.

04 — Safety

Five rules for home and the workshop

  1. Only power up the robot in an enclosed arena or with the weapon disconnected. Switch on the transmitter first, then the robot; switch off in reverse order.
  2. Check the failsafe before the first match: switch off the transmitter and the robot must stop.
  3. Charge LiPo batteries only with a suitable charger, under supervision, away from flammable objects and ideally in a safety bag. Never use a damaged or swollen cell.
  4. A flipper moves violently. Keep your fingers out of its path when handling the robot.
  5. Kits are for ages 14 and up, younger children only under adult supervision.
05 — Steps

From the browser to your first tournament

  1. Play a few matches in the arena, for example in the training hall without a time limit.
  2. Read the antweight rules.
  3. Build a pusher and weigh it. Keep a few grams in reserve for repairs.
  4. Test the failsafe, the switch and the 15 × 15 cm size.
  5. Follow the tournaments on the organiser’s website and sign up.
