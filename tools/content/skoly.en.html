01 — What you can use today

Three tools, all free and without sign-in.

  • SO-101 simulation

    10 missions with a robot arm

    MuJoCo physics simulation. Two control modes: Smart move (drag the blue ball and the program works out the joints) and Joints (each joint on its own slider, angles in degrees). Each mission earns 1–3 stars for time and precision. The Learning from demonstration panel records a movement and plays it back.

  • 150 g arena

    Combat robots in the browser

    A quick match against the computer, a tournament, two players on one keyboard and a training hall without a time limit. In the garage students change wheels, gears and weapons, and the weight has to stay within 150 g. Physics with mass and friction.

02 — Ready lesson · 45 minutes

How does a robot know where to reach?

Overview
Who forLower and upper secondary school (roughly ages 11–19), clubs. No previous robotics experience needed.
SubjectsComputing (algorithms, modelling), design and technology, physics. How it fits your curriculum is up to you; we have not mapped it to a national curriculum yet.
You needA computer or tablet with an up-to-date browser for every 1–2 students, a projector, a printed worksheet.
PreparationAbout 15 minutes: play missions 01 and 03 yourself and open the simulation once on the school network and on a student device.
Goals

By the end of the lesson, students can:

  1. name the joints of a robot arm and describe how moving one joint changes where the gripper is;
  2. write a pick-and-place task as a sequence of steps (an algorithm) and test it in the simulation;
  3. explain the difference between controlling joint by joint and “smart move”, where the program works out the joint angles (inverse kinematics);
  4. describe learning from demonstration: the robot repeats a demonstrated movement, but without more demonstrations it can’t cope with a change.

03 — Safety and data

Things to keep in mind

  • The simulation carries no physical risk. Sound starts switched off in both the arena and the simulation. The arena’s Calm mode turns off camera shake, slow-motion replays, the crowd and the jingles. To give students a link with it already on, use printedrobotics.cz/sim/arena/?klid=1.
  • Accounts and data: students enter nothing and sign up nowhere. Mission progress is saved only in the browser of that device. We do not send or collect students’ results.
  • Real kits (if you go further): for ages 14 and up, younger children only under adult supervision. Moving parts can pinch fingers. Power the hardware only from the supply specified in the project instructions.
  • Combat robots: charge lithium batteries only with a suitable charger and under supervision. Only power up a robot in an enclosed arena and check the failsafe before the first match (when the transmitter is switched off, the robot must stop). We don’t recommend robots with spinning weapons for lessons. More in the guide Getting started with 150 g combat robots.

Connect AI: a teacher-led projector activity

In the SO-101 simulation an AI assistant, Claude, can also drive the arm (the Connect AI button). Claude accounts are for adults (18+), so this is an activity the teacher leads: you connect your own Claude on your computer and show the simulation on the projector, and the class tells it what the AI should do. Students do not connect an AI on their own devices. Prompts like “explain it and let me try” from What to tell the AI work well: the AI describes the approach and a student does the next step.

05 — Questions

Teachers’ questions

How much does it cost?

The SO-101 simulation, the arena, the servo setup wizard and this lesson with its worksheet are free. We don’t sell real robots: the school buys the parts directly from suppliers.

Do students need to sign up?

No. Students enter nothing. Mission progress is saved only in the browser of the device they use, and we don’t send results anywhere.

Which devices does the simulation work on?

An up-to-date Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari on a computer, tablet or phone. The browser needs WebGL and WebAssembly. We have not tested school Chromebooks yet, so try one device beforehand. The first load downloads the physics engine and 3D models and takes longer on a slow network.

Can I send students a link straight to one mission?

Yes. Every mission has its own link, for example printedrobotics.cz/sim/so101/?mise=sort&lang=en for sorting colours. The links are in the mission table above. You can also send students a recorded demonstration as a link (Share the demo as a link).

How do I see students’ results?

The simulation doesn’t collect results. Students write their time and stars on the worksheet. After completing a mission they can also copy a link with their time (Share your record) and send it to you.

Do you also have real robots for schools?

The catalogue has open projects, such as the SO-101 robot arm, the Otto DIY walking robot or the Project SVRN combat robot. We sell nothing: for the SO-101 and Project SVRN a buying plan shows what to buy from which suppliers; for the others we link to the project’s bill of materials. Building is for ages 14 and up, younger children under adult supervision.
