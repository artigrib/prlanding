SO-101 assembly, step by step

Build a pair of SO-101 arms (the leader you guide by hand and the follower that copies it) from unboxing to first motion. Seven stages, each with a time estimate, difficulty, checklist and what to do when it doesn’t work.

How to read this guide. Steps, screws and commands come from the official LeRobot guide and the SO-ARM100 repository (as of 29 Oct 2026). Additions that are not in the official sources are marked our note. All times are estimates: we don’t have a documented build of our own yet and will update them from it. Where this guide and the official documentation differ, the official documentation wins.

0 — Before you build

Tools, workspace and a servo check

Time {bt:e0} h (estimate)Difficulty easy

Tools and workspace

  • Phillips screwdrivers #0 and #1. The SO-ARM100 repository recommends exactly these for the arm’s screws.2
  • A small flat screwdriver or putty knife for removing supports.1
  • Table clamps: the bill of materials lists 4 for a pair (2 for one arm).2
  • Our note: a tray or a box with compartments for screws, good light, a table you can clamp the arm to later, and a computer with USB-C.

Servo variants: check them right after unboxing

All servos are Feetech STS3215, but the gear ratio differs. The follower has six identical servos; the leader has three different ratios so it is easy to guide by hand yet holds its own weight.1 A mix-up is the most common mistake, and it only shows once the arm is assembled.

2Shoulder (shoulder_lift)1/345 (C001)1/345 (C018)1/345 (C001)
3Elbow (elbow_flex)1/345 (C001)1/345 (C018)1/191 (C044)
4Wrist pitch (wrist_flex)1/345 (C001)1/345 (C018)1/147 (C046)

Ratios from the official LeRobot guide, codes C001, C044 and C046 from the SO-ARM100 bill of materials, C018 from the maker’s number on the shop page.1, 2 For a pair with the standard follower: 7× C001 (1/345), 2× C044 (1/191), 3× C046 (1/147). With a 12 V follower: 6× C018, and for the leader 1× C001, 2× C044, 3× C046. The buying plan covers both.

Label them as you unpack. Keep every parcel of servos separate and label each servo straight away: the code from the listing or box, L/F and the joint number. We haven’t yet checked on real units whether the code or ratio is printed on the servo itself; if it is, check that too. We will add a photo of the marking after our own build.

Voltage: the 7.4 V servos run from a 5 V supply. The follower can also be built with stronger 12 V servos (C018); it then needs a 12 V supply rated at least 5 A. The SO-101 leader always uses 7.4 V servos.2 Never mix 12 V and 7.4 V servos on one bus or one power supply.

Checklist

  • Every parcel of servos kept separate, servos labelled (code, L/F, joint number from the table).
  • The supply matches the servos: 5 V for 7.4 V servos, 12 V (5 A+) only for 12 V follower servos.
  • 2 servo driver boards, 2 USB-C cables, clamps.
  • Phillips #0 and #1 screwdrivers. Screws for a pair: {sc:pair_m2}× M2×6 and up to {sc:pair_m3}× M3×6 (table in stage 3).
  • You’ve looked through the exploded view or the Assembly animation.

When it doesn’t work

  • I can’t tell the gear ratio. Don’t guess from the looks; the servos look the same. Ask the seller or the kit supplier.
  • Screws are missing. The SO-ARM100 bill of materials doesn’t list screws separately; the official guide only uses M2×6 and M3×6.1, 2 We haven’t confirmed that the screws come with the servos. The table in stage 3 shows how many each arm needs; as a backup, buy an assortment of M2×6 and M3×6 Phillips screws with some spares.
1 — Printing

Printing the parts and removing supports

Time ≈ {bt:print} h printer + {bt:e1} h work (estimate)Difficulty medium

Recommended settings from the SO-ARM100 repository:2

  • Material PLA+, infill 15%.
  • 0.4 mm nozzle at 0.2 mm layers, or 0.6 mm nozzle at 0.4 mm layers.
  • Supports everywhere, except slopes steeper than 45° to the horizontal. No supports in screw holes with a horizontal axis.
  • No need to orient anything: all leader or follower parts are in a single file, already oriented Z-up to minimise supports. There are files for 220 × 220 mm beds (Ender) and 205 × 250 mm beds (Prusa).

Gauges first. The Gauges folder has test prints: a gauge for the STS3215 servo (Gauge 0 and Gauge tight) or for a 4×2 Lego brick. If gauge 0 doesn’t fit, fix your printer settings before printing a whole arm.2 Servos not here yet? Print the Lego gauge: almost every home has a 4×2 brick, and printing doesn’t have to wait for the parcel.

What to print

Paths in the SO-ARM100 repository (checked 30 Oct 2026). Pick one of each pair of gauges and one arm file per arm to suit your bed:2

  • Servo gauge: STL/Gauges/Gauge_0.STL and Gauge_tight_1.STL; or Lego: Lego_Size_Test_02_zero.STL and Lego_Size_Test_02_minuspoint1.STL.
  • Follower: STL/SO101/Follower/Ender_Follower_SO101.stl (220 × 220 mm bed) or Prusa_Follower_SO101.stl (205 × 250 mm).
  • Leader: STL/SO101/Leader/Ender_Leader_SO101.stl or Prusa_Leader_SO101.stl.
  • Plate for the driver board, 1 per arm: STL/SO101/Individual/WaveShare_Mounting_Plate_SO101.stl (for the Seeed board Seeedstudio_Mounting_Plate_SO101.stl). The repository lists it with the common parts; we haven’t checked whether the whole-arm file contains it, so plan it as a separate print.

For Bambu Lab A1 mini printers the same folders have BambuLabA1mini_…_SO101.stl files. Single parts are in STL/SO101/Individual.

Checklist

  • Printer calibrated, bed clean and dry.
  • Gauge 0 fits the servo (or Lego brick) as in the repository’s video.
  • Leader and follower files printed for your bed size, plus 2 plates for the driver boards.
  • Supports removed, screw holes and servo pockets clean.

When it doesn’t work

  • The servo won’t go in, or sits loose. Print the gauge again and adjust your printer’s dimensional accuracy. Our note: don’t sand or drill the pocket blindly, or only the screws will hold the servo.
  • Support left in a hole. Pick it out carefully with a small screwdriver.1 Our note: don’t drill the hole bigger, an M2 screw won’t hold in it.
  • No printer. The repository links to printing services; other sellers also sell printed parts (the buying plan shows the options). We don’t print or sell parts ourselves.
2 — Servo IDs

Every servo gets its own ID, before assembly

Time {bt:e2} h including installing LeRobot (estimate)Difficulty easy

LeRobot sets up the real servos with lerobot-setup-motors (below). Dry run: you can go through the whole process (port, IDs, calibration, test) first in the servo setup dry run. Out of the box the emulated servos all have ID 1, like the real ones, and the wizard can simulate typical faults too.

Installation: Python 3.12 and LeRobot 0.6.1 from PyPI. All our SO-101 guides use the same steps (assembly, first steps and simulation); we pin the version we tested against the virtual arm.3, 1

python3.12 -m venv .venv-lerobot
source .venv-lerobot/bin/activate
pip install "lerobot[feetech,core-scripts]==0.6.1"

Windows: py -3.12 -m venv .venv-lerobot and .venv-lerobot\Scripts\activate. The official guide recommends a conda environment (Miniforge); that works the same way, with conda create -n lerobot python=3.12 instead of venv. You need ffmpeg only later, for recording camera video, not for the first motion (Linux sudo apt install ffmpeg, macOS brew install ffmpeg).

Find the port of each driver board with lerobot-find-port (it asks you to unplug the USB cable). On Linux it looks like /dev/ttyACM0, on macOS /dev/tty.usbmodem…, on Windows COM3. In the examples below we write /dev/ttyACM0 for the follower and /dev/ttyACM1 for the leader: replace them with yours. On Linux you may need to grant access to the port: sudo chmod 666 /dev/ttyACM0.1

Our note on Windows: if no new COM port shows up in Device Manager after you plug in the board, the USB-to-serial driver is missing. Waveshare boards use WCH chips (CH343 or CH340); we haven’t checked which one your board has, so read the marking on the chip and get the driver from WCH.

Then the follower and the leader (only the one you are setting up is connected):1

lerobot-setup-motors \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0      # follower port from lerobot-find-port

lerobot-setup-motors \
    --teleop.type=so101_leader \
    --teleop.port=/dev/ttyACM1     # leader port from lerobot-find-port

Before writing an ID, LeRobot only checks that it is talking to an STS3215; it cannot tell the voltage or the gear ratio. Keep track of the variant with the labels from stage 0.

The script prompts “Connect the controller board to the 'gripper' motor only and press enter.” Connect only one servo, with nothing else daisy-chained to it. It starts with the gripper (ID 6) and ends with the base rotation (ID 1).1

Checklist

  • LeRobot installed, the port of both boards written down.
  • On a Waveshare board both jumpers are on channel B (USB).1
  • All 6 follower servos have IDs 6 to 1 and a numbered label.
  • All 6 leader servos have IDs 6 to 1 and a numbered label. Don’t mix leader and follower.
  • One 3-pin cable plugged into every servo (the official guide recommends this before assembly).1

The step images are our renders of the SO-101 CAD model (The Robot Studio, SO-ARM100, via MuJoCo Menagerie, Apache License 2.0) and show the follower. Screws, the driver board and the cable are schematic symbols; the numbers in circles refer to the screw legend in the image.

When it doesn’t work

  • Error while writing the ID. Check the power supply, the USB cable and the 3-pin cable to the servo. Between steps, check that the supply didn’t come loose while you handled the board.1
  • The Waveshare board doesn’t respond. The jumpers must be on channel B (USB).1
  • Several servos answer, or none. More than one servo was connected, or a servo was still daisy-chained. You can try this fault (Duplicate ID) in the dry run.
  • Servos from another robot. They have a different ID and bus speed; set them up again with this step.1
3 — Mechanics

Joint by joint: base, shoulder, elbow, wrist, gripper

Time {bt:e3_arm} h per arm, {bt:e3} h per pair (estimate)Difficulty hard

Steps and screws are from the official guide, which has a short video for each joint.1 Play an animation in the official order on the SO-101 page with the Assembly button, or in the simulation under “What the arm is made of”.

General rules. The official guide uses just two screw sizes: M2×6 (the smallest) mostly to hold servos and holders, and M3×6 for the horns (the servo’s rotating disc) and the parts fixed to them. On servos with two horns only the top one gets a screw; the bottom horn has none.1

Screws per arm

How many screws each step uses, added up from the steps of the official guide.1 The buying plan and the checklist use the same table.

{sc:table}

For a pair: {sc:pair_m2}× M2×6 and up to {sc:pair_m3}× M3×6. {sc:counting} {sc:included} Keep some spares: small screws get lost easily.

Joint 1: base rotation (ID 1)

  • Fit both horns and secure the top one with an M3×6.
  • Place the servo in the base and fix it with 4× M2×6 (two from the top, two from the bottom).
  • Slide on the first motor holder, 2× M2×6 (one on each side).
  • Fit the shoulder part and tighten 4× M3×6 on top and 4× M3×6 underneath. Add the shoulder motor holder.

Joint 2: shoulder (ID 2)

  • Both horns, top one M3×6. Slide the servo in from the top and fix it with 4× M2×6.
  • Attach the upper arm with 4× M3×6 on each side.

Joint 3: elbow (ID 3)

  • Both horns, top one M3×6. Insert servo 3 and fix it with 4× M2×6.
  • Attach the forearm to servo 3 with 4× M3×6 on each side.

Joint 4: wrist pitch (ID 4)

  • Both horns, top one M3×6. First slide on motor holder 4, then slide in servo 4 and fix it with 4× M2×6.

Joint 5: wrist roll (ID 5)

  • Put servo 5 into the wrist holder, 2× M2×6 from the front.
  • This servo takes only one horn; secure it with an M3×6.
  • Fix the wrist to servo 4 with M3×6 screws on both sides (official wording: “4 M3x6mm screws on both sides”).

Follower gripper (ID 6)

  • Attach the gripper to the wrist horn with 4× M3×6.
  • Insert the gripper servo, 2× M2×6 on each side. Both horns, top one M3×6.
  • Attach the moving jaw with 4× M3×6 on both sides.

Leader handle (ID 6)

  • Leader holder onto the wrist with 4× M3×6, handle to the holder with 1× M2×6.
  • Insert the servo, 2× M2×6 on each side, horn M3×6. Attach the trigger with 4× M3×6.

Checklist (for each arm)

  • Every servo is in the joint with the right ID and gear ratio (leader: 1/191, 1/345, 1/191, 1/147, 1/147, 1/147).
  • On every servo with two horns the top one has an M3×6, the bottom one is free.
  • Servo 5 has only one horn.
  • Cables run freely and nothing gets pinched when you move each joint end to end (by hand, unpowered).
  • No screw is longer than 6 mm and none is over-tightened into the plastic.
  • Screws as in the table: follower {sc:f_m2}× M2×6 and up to {sc:f_m3}× M3×6, leader {sc:l_m2}× M2×6 and up to {sc:l_m3}× M3×6.

When it doesn’t work

  • The leader is stiff or droops by itself. Most likely a servo is swapped: check the ratios against the table in stage 0. A leader with 1/345 servos everywhere is hard to move by hand.
  • Horn and zero position. The official guide doesn’t prescribe a horn zero position during assembly: the middle of the range is set by calibration (stage 5), when LeRobot writes an offset to the servo so that the middle of the joint is half a turn.5 Our note: what matters is that the horn sits fully on the shaft splines and isn’t fitted at an angle.
  • A cable snags or pops out. Plug the cable into the servo before sliding the servo into the part, as the official guide recommends.1 Our note: leave a small loop at each joint so the cable doesn’t pull on the connector as it moves.
  • Stripped plastic. Our note: the M2 and M3 screws cut their own thread in the plastic. Tighten until you feel resistance, not by force. A stripped hole usually means reprinting the part.
  • A screw won’t tighten or something rubs. Our note: check the length; a screw longer than 6 mm can hit the inside of the servo or break through the plastic.
4 — Electronics

Servo chain, driver board and power

Time {bt:e4} h (estimate)Difficulty medium

Once the IDs are set, daisy-chain the servos: a 3-pin cable from each servo to the next, and the cable from the first servo (base rotation, ID 1) to the driver board. Then attach the board to the arm’s base.1 The repository has a printed plate for the Waveshare board (WaveShare_Mounting_Plate).2

Power: each arm has its own board and its own supply. 7.4 V servos on a 5 V supply, 12 V follower servos on a 12 V supply (5 A or more).2 Our note: never connect a 12 V supply to an arm with 7.4 V servos; it is above their rated voltage.

Connector: the Waveshare board has a DC 5.5 × 2.1 mm power jack and screw terminals next to it. The maker states a 9–12.6 V input “matching the servo voltage”; the official SO-101 bill of materials still powers the 7.4 V servos from a 5 V 4 A supply with a 5.5 × 2.1 mm plug, and ready-made kits do the same.2, 6 A 12 V 5 A supply for the 12 V follower is within the board’s range. The supply powers the servos; USB only carries the data.

Checklist

  • Chain: board → ID 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6, all connectors clicked in.
  • Waveshare jumpers on channel B.
  • The supply matches the servos (5 V or 12 V) and is plugged into that arm’s board.
  • The supply is within reach so you can pull it at any time.

When it doesn’t work

  • Some servo doesn’t respond. A loose cable in the chain cuts off every servo after it. Try the “Loose cable” fault with diagnostics in the dry run.
  • The supply’s plug doesn’t fit the board. The jack is 5.5 × 2.1 mm; 5.5 × 2.5 mm plugs sit loose or only work now and then. Use a supply with a 5.5 × 2.1 mm plug, or a jack-to-screw-terminal adapter (5.5 × 2.1 mm socket on a terminal block) and wire it to the board’s terminals. Check the polarity marked at the terminals: plus to plus. Our note: never use a supply with a different voltage from the servos, even if the plug fits.
  • Servos reset or weaken under load. Our note: check that the supply matches the bill of materials and that its connector holds; the dry run has a “Wrong power supply” fault for this.
5 — First motion

Calibration and teleoperation

Time {bt:e5} h (estimate)Difficulty medium

Clamp the arms to the table. Calibration makes the leader and follower report the same values in the same physical position.1 Follower:

# Use the port of your follower arm, and give the arm a unique name with --robot.id
lerobot-calibrate \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 \
    --robot.id=my_awesome_follower_arm

Leader:

# Use the port of your leader arm, and give the arm a unique name with --teleop.id
lerobot-calibrate \
    --teleop.type=so101_leader \
    --teleop.port=/dev/ttyACM1 \
    --teleop.id=my_awesome_leader_arm

The procedure: first move all joints to the middle of their range, press Enter, then move each joint through its full range.1 According to the LeRobot code, wrist roll isn’t calibrated by range; it gets the full turn.5 Then teleoperate:4

lerobot-teleoperate \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 \
    --robot.id=my_awesome_follower_arm \
    --teleop.type=so101_leader \
    --teleop.port=/dev/ttyACM1 \
    --teleop.id=my_awesome_leader_arm

Replace /dev/ttyACM0 (follower) and /dev/ttyACM1 (leader) with your ports from stage 2 (macOS /dev/tty.usbmodem…, Windows COM3 and so on). The official guide’s examples use the same port for both arms; each arm has its own. Use the same arm IDs in every later command.

Only a follower? Teleoperation needs a leader. After calibrating the follower you can move it by replaying recorded motion, for example an episode from the guide LeRobot against a virtual arm:

lerobot-replay \
    --robot.type=so101_follower --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 --robot.id=my_awesome_follower_arm \
    --dataset.repo_id=local/so101_sil --dataset.root=$HOME/so101_sil_dataset --dataset.episode=0

Our note: we haven’t yet tested replaying a virtual-arm dataset on a real arm. The first time, hold the arm, keep the power supply within reach and keep the arm’s reach clear. Your own demonstrations and training need a leader.

Dry run with the real LeRobot: you can go through calibration, teleoperation and recording a dataset against a virtual arm before you build, see Before the parts arrive: LeRobot against a virtual arm. Use different IDs for the virtual arms than for the real ones.

Checklist

  • Both arms clamped to the table, nothing within the follower’s reach.
  • Follower and leader calibrated, each under its own permanent ID.
  • The follower copies the leader in all six joints, gripper included.
  • You know how to stop the arm: pull the servo supply.

When it doesn’t work

  • The arm drops during calibration. Servo torque is off during calibration; hold the arm.
  • A joint moves the wrong way or only a little during teleoperation. Repeat calibration and really move each joint from stop to stop. The dry run has this as the “Bad calibration” fault.
  • A servo gets hot. The joint is pushing against something or sitting at its stop. Remove the obstacle. LeRobot limits the follower gripper to half torque so the servo doesn’t burn out.5
  • Still stuck. The official guide points to the LeRobot community on Discord.1

Safety: the kits are for ages 14 and up, younger children only under adult supervision.
